How far back does a mushroom remember the weather?

Published estimates of the look-back window between weather and fruiting, gathered from field studies and searchable by region and date. Pick a place and a day to see only the studies that apply, with each window turned into calendar dates.

These windows come from different methods, time steps and things measured (counts, yield, fruiting date). They are not interchangeable parameters. Each one is tied to the site where it was measured, so using it anywhere else is a hypothesis for your own data to test.

Ways to sharpen the tool

Each item comes from a source you can check. The order is by how much honesty it buys per unit of effort.

  1. Test against the calendar. A model scored against random dates looks good just by learning the season. The winter chanterelle model below reports AUC of 0.81 or more on held-out years, scored that way. Always report skill over a day-of-year baseline too.
  2. Learn the windows, then try to break them. Sliding-window searches (the climwin package) test every window and include randomisation checks for false positives, which matter when many windows are compared. Bailey and van de Pol 2016
  3. Use temporal pseudo-absences and a benchmark taxon. Same coordinates, random dates, plus a group with no visible season (pines) to estimate when observers are out. In that test the correction changed skill very little, so treat it as a check, not a cure. bioRxiv preprint, 2023
  4. Model detection apart from presence. The Kyoto study fitted a zero-inflated model with a separate detection probability, so an empty survey is not read as an absent fungus. Sato et al. 2012
  5. Combine messy records with structured plots. Integrated distribution models do this, but simulation shows integration alone does not fix spatial bias. A bias covariate or a flexible spatial term is needed. Simmonds et al. 2020
  6. Map the mycelium, not only the mushroom. GlobalFungi holds soil DNA records from 57,184 geolocated samples (release 4, CC BY 4.0). It can say the fungus is present where nobody has photographed a fruit body. globalfungi.com
  7. Add last year's tree growth and satellite soil moisture. In Soria, previous-year NDVI and radar soil moisture lifted yield models to an adjusted R² of up to 0.63. Olano et al. 2020
  8. Carry a memory of last year's crop. In the Yukon, a big year was never followed by another, whatever the rain did. Krebs et al. 2008
  9. Fit per species. A verification study found species-level networks beat genus-level ones. Scientific Reports 2024
  10. Never draw finer than the evidence. Post-fire morels clustered at scales under 7 m, far below any open weather or soil grid. Larson et al. 2016
