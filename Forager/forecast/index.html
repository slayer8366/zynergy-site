Mushroom forecast: Pacific Northwest pilot

An early look at the weekly chance of chanterelles being reported, cell by cell, from the Oregon–California border to the Canadian border. It's a pilot: the banner says how it did when tested on years it hadn't seen. It opens on the soil layer. Choose Sighting chance (pilot) in the layer list to see the forecast.

How to read these layers

Read each layer on its own. They are not added up into a score or a hot spot, and a strong colour on any of them is not a place to go.

Soil pH, top 30 cm

What it shows
How acid or alkaline the top 30 cm of soil is. Lower numbers are more acid.
Why a forager might care
  • Chanterelles: no source in the project yet links soil pH to them.
  • Chicken of the woods: no source in the project yet links soil pH to it.
  • It is here because the habitat model will test it.
Limits
  • A prediction from SoilGrids 2.0 (published 2021), not a soil test.
  • Each square is 250 m and shows the average over it, not any one spot in it.
  • A model input, not a forecast. It says nothing about this week.
  • Public land and access are not shown, and access isn't permission to harvest.

Tree canopy cover

What it shows
How much of each square is under live tree canopy, in percent.
Why a forager might care
  • Chanterelles: in the Pacific Northwest they "generally associate with Douglas-fir, hemlock, spruce, fir, and pine" (Pilz et al. 2003, p. 19).
  • Chicken of the woods: in the West one species grows on conifers and another on hardwoods, mainly oak and eucalyptus (Burdsall and Banik 2001).
  • Cover alone does not say which trees. The two share layers do, for Douglas-fir and hemlock.
Limits
  • From TreeMap 2023, itself "a tree-level model of the forests" of the United States "circa 2023".
  • Squares that are mostly lake or sea are blank.
  • In Canada the cover is SCANFI v2's total crown closure (2025), a different measure from TreeMap's, so a step in colour at the border can come from the two sources rather than the forest.
  • Each square is 250 m and shows the average over it, not any one spot in it.
  • A model input, not a forecast. It says nothing about this week.
  • Public land and access are not shown, and access isn't permission to harvest.

Douglas-fir share of canopy

What it shows
Of the tree canopy in each square, the part that is Douglas-fir.
Why a forager might care
  • Chanterelles: Douglas-fir is the first tree Pilz et al. (2003) name among their Pacific Northwest associates.
  • Chicken of the woods: Douglas-fir is a conifer, and the western conifer species occurs "on mature and over mature living and dead conifers" (Burdsall and Banik 2001).
Limits
  • A share, not an amount: half of a thin canopy is fewer trees than half of a dense forest. Look at tree cover beside it; the map does not combine them.
  • Blank where canopy cover is under 10%. From TreeMap 2023 (circa 2023) in the US and SCANFI v2 (2025) in Canada.
  • Each square is 250 m and shows the average over it, not any one spot in it.
  • A model input, not a forecast. It says nothing about this week.
  • Public land and access are not shown, and access isn't permission to harvest.

Hemlock share of canopy

What it shows
Of the tree canopy in each square, the part that is hemlock.
Why a forager might care
  • Chanterelles: hemlock is among the Pacific Northwest associates Pilz et al. (2003) name, and the Pacific golden chanterelle occurs "under hemlock, Douglas-fir, and spruce".
  • Chicken of the woods: hemlock is a conifer, and the western conifer species occurs "on mature and over mature living and dead conifers" (Burdsall and Banik 2001).
Limits
  • A share, not an amount: half of a thin canopy is fewer trees than half of a dense forest. Look at tree cover beside it; the map does not combine them.
  • Blank where canopy cover is under 10%. From TreeMap 2023 (circa 2023).
  • Not available in Canada: the Canadian source does not separate hemlock from other conifers, so Canada is blank on this layer. Blank there means not known, not none.
  • Each square is 250 m and shows the average over it, not any one spot in it.
  • A model input, not a forecast. It says nothing about this week.
  • Public land and access are not shown, and access isn't permission to harvest.

Sighting chance (pilot)

What it shows
For each 0.1 degree weather cell (about 11 km north to south and 8 km east to west), a pilot model's sighting chance for chanterelles for one week, named in the banner: the chance chanterelles are reported in this cell that week, given someone reported any fungus here that week.
Why a forager might care
  • Chanterelles: it is the first number on this page that reads recent weather, not only soil and trees. The banner says if a run is calendar only, with no weather.
  • Chicken of the woods: not in the pilot.
  • It is here so the pilot can be looked at and questioned before the full model is built.
Limits
  • A pilot. Not validated. Its figures were checked by an independent review. The banner above the map says whether it beat the seasonal calendar on years it was not fitted to. It is shown even if it did not.
  • It counts reports, not mushrooms. A cell where few people look or post can read low whatever is growing there.
  • A cell with no colour has no forecast: it was not scored, or the model does not apply there. It is not a low chance. The banner says how many cells were scored.
  • One number per cell, for the whole cell. It says nothing about any one spot in it, and it is not shaded by the soil and tree layers on this page.
  • The Pacific Northwest strip only, 42 to 49.5 N, 121 to 125 W.
  • Public land and access are not shown, and access isn't permission to harvest.

What this is, and what it is not

These are two of the inputs a forecast will use: the soil's acidity and the trees mushrooms grow with. Each square is 250 m. They are shown here so they can be looked at and questioned. They say nothing about this week, and nothing about whether mushrooms are there.

The forecast is a weekly sighting chance per mushroom group and weather cell. This pilot covers chanterelles only. It is shown together with the result of its test against a seasonal calendar on years it was not fitted to, whether or not it passed, so it can be looked at and questioned.

Box: 40 to 49 N, 111 to 125 W. Tree data on the Canadian side comes from its own source, SCANFI v2, which maps Douglas-fir but not hemlock.
