Privacy Policy for Forager

Last updated: 29 September 2026  ·  Applies to the Forager Android app (com.zynergylabs.forager.app), including its Google Play closed test.

This is the document Google Play's Data safety declaration points at. It is written to match what the app's code actually does.

The short version

Forager has no account, no sign-in, no sync and no analytics. Nothing you record, meaning tracks, journal entries, photos, offline map regions and settings, is uploaded to us or to anyone else.

What is transmitted, while your phone is online, is where you are looking: the coordinates a search runs on, and the map tiles for the area on screen. Those go to iNaturalist, Open-Meteo, the map tile providers, and a Cloudflare Worker we operate. They carry a location and your IP address, and the services that receive them may log them.

Because location leaves the device and reaches third parties, the Play Data safety declaration for this app states that location is collected and shared. That is the accurate description, not a conservative one.

What stays on your device

Stored in the app's own private storage, readable by no other app:

  • Recorded tracks, with their names and start and end times, and their individual GPS points: latitude, longitude, timestamp, elevation, accuracy, speed and speed accuracy.
  • Waypoints you drop: position, altitude, name, note and time.
  • Planned trips: a name, a place and a date.
  • Journal and log entries, including any coordinate attached to a find. A journal entry also keeps its own copy of the coordinates of the waypoints and regions it lists.
  • Photos taken in the app or imported from your gallery, with the place and time on record for each (see Photos and location metadata).
  • Downloaded offline map regions, with each region's name, centre and radius.
  • Your last five searches (the place, radius, month and filter, and the species list that came back), so that a search still answers offline. A new search replaces the one used least recently.
  • Preferences such as units, and the centre and radius you last chose for an offline region.

Crash traces are kept apart from the rest. When the app crashes it writes a trace (the time, the thread, the Android version number and the stack trace) to Forager's own folder in the phone's shared storage, and keeps the newest ten. That folder is outside the app's private storage on purpose, so the phone's file manager can open it; on older Android versions, other apps allowed to read shared storage can open it too. A trace leaves the phone only if you share it from Settings → Crash Logs.

The app uploads none of this. Something leaves the phone only when you send it yourself: a track you export, a crash trace you share, or a trip or waypoint you open in a maps app (see below). All of it is deleted when you uninstall Forager or clear its data.

Android's own backup is switched off

The app sets android:allowBackup="false", which turns off both Android Auto Backup and the device-to-device transfer that runs during a new phone's setup. Your phone therefore does not copy Forager's data to your Google account, and does not hand it to another device on your behalf.

This is a permanent decision rather than a setting for the closed test. Moving your data belongs to the app, not to the operating system.

Exporting a track

You can export a recorded track as a GPX file from Records → Recorded Tracks. Android's share sheet sends it wherever you choose, and once shared, that copy is outside Forager. The file holds every point the track stored, with its time, elevation, accuracy and speed, marked as kept or excluded along with the reason; and the waypoints dropped while recording, with their names, notes and times.

A copy of each exported file also stays in the app's cache folder. Exporting the same track again replaces that copy rather than adding another.

Journal entries and photos cannot be exported, and there is no import.

As with any HTTP request, each of these also reveals your IP address and ordinary request metadata to the recipient. Each recipient's own privacy policy governs what it then does with that.

One thing the app does not request itself: tapping an observation to open it on iNaturalist hands a link to your browser or to the iNaturalist app. From that point it is an ordinary web visit made by that other app, under its own terms, not a request Forager makes.

The same goes for Directions on a planned trip or a waypoint: Forager hands its name and coordinates to the maps app you pick, and what happens next is that app's request, not Forager's.

Downloading an offline region issues a large number of tile requests in a short time, covering the whole region. That is the download. Having a region downloaded does not stop the Maps tab loading its map tiles online, inside the region too, and searching still sends its requests.

The map tile service we operate

The forager-pmtiles.brandonlee1-894.workers.dev endpoint is operated by us on Cloudflare's platform, serving tiles out of a Cloudflare R2 bucket. Your device's requests go to the Worker, not to a tile vendor.

One level of detail is not in our own archive. When a tile at that level (zoom 15) is asked for and we do not have it yet, the Worker fetches it from Protomaps' public map build (build.protomaps.com) and keeps a copy in the R2 bucket. That fetch is made by the Worker, from Cloudflare, and carries nothing from your device's request, so Protomaps learns that the tile was wanted but not who wanted it.

We keep no logs of these requests. The Worker's own code writes no log lines, and request logging is explicitly disabled in its configuration rather than left to a platform default. Two things are kept that are not logs: Cloudflare caches the tiles it serves, keyed by the tile's address, and the zoom 15 tiles fetched from Protomaps stay in the R2 bucket. Neither records who asked for a tile.

Cloudflare operates the infrastructure and, as the host, processes these requests on our behalf under its own privacy policy. We state no retention period here because there is no setting of ours that produces one, and we will not publish a figure we cannot verify.

Nothing about these requests is linked to an account, because the app has none. Nothing is sold, shared for advertising, or used for profiling.

Photos and location metadata

Photos stay on your phone. The app never transmits one. What the app does with the location metadata embedded in a photo file is stated here as it actually is:

  • A photo taken with Forager's camera (the app's own camera, not a separate camera app) is stripped of its embedded metadata when it is saved: location, camera details, times, thumbnails and the rest. What is kept is what the picture needs to display: its orientation, written back after the strip, its colour profile, and two small technical headers. If the strip fails, the photo is kept unchanged and the failure is logged.
  • A photo you import is stored as Android hands it over. Forager does not strip it and does not ask Android for the original file. On Android 10 and later, Android normally removes GPS tags from the copy an app is given. On Android 8 and 9, which the app still supports, it does not, so an imported photo's stored copy can keep its GPS tags.

The place and time of a photo are kept in the app's own database instead:

  • For a photo taken with Forager's camera, your position when you took it, if Automatically Save Location to Photos is on in Settings. It starts on. Switching it off stops every automatic position capture, for photos and for finds alike; a place you set yourself is still kept.
  • For an imported photo, the date and location Forager reads from the original file, using the ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION permission below. That reading goes into the database only; the stored copy is not changed.

Forager has no way to share or export a photo, so a photo's file does not leave the phone through the app.

Permissions and what they are used for

  • Location (ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION, ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION): showing your position, recording a track, navigating back, the coordinates a species or weather lookup searches on, and, while Automatically Save Location to Photos is on, the place a photo or find was made. Forager asks Android for both GPS and network-based positions. While the app is in the foreground it receives location updates continuously, not only while you are recording. When you leave the app, it releases its location subscription rather than relying on Android to withhold it.
  • Camera (CAMERA): taking a photo for a journal entry, with Forager's own camera.
  • ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION: reading the capture date and coordinate of a photo you import, so a find can be dated and placed. Read separately from the stored copy's bytes.
  • Notifications, vibrate, foreground service (POST_NOTIFICATIONS, VIBRATE, FOREGROUND_SERVICE): the off-track alert, the sundown alerts (a turnaround warning and one at sunset, while a track is recording), and the recording notification. The sundown alerts are computed on the device from the clock and your position; nothing is sent anywhere to produce them.
  • Internet (INTERNET): the requests listed above.
  • Network and Wi-Fi state (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, ACCESS_WIFI_STATE): added by MapLibre, the library Forager draws its maps with. Forager's own manifest does not declare them.

On Android 13 and later, the notification permission is asked for when you start a recording.

Forager does not ask for background location. The permission Android calls ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION is not requested and not declared. A recording keeps running with your screen off because it runs as a foreground service, with the ongoing notification you can see, not because the app can read your location in the background.

Outside those two states, app open or a recording running, Forager receives no location at all.

You can revoke location permission at any time in Android Settings. Recording will not work without it, but the rest of the app will. Revoking it stops Forager reading your position; it does not stop the requests listed above, because a search on coordinates you type in, and the map you move, still send them.

No analytics, no ads, no tracking

The app bundles no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising SDK. There is no telemetry of any kind: no usage events, no session data, no device fingerprint, no third-party identifier. Beyond the map requests our tile service receives (above), nothing about your use of the app reaches us unless you write it in a message and send it yourself.

Beta testing

If you are using Forager as a closed beta tester, everything above applies to you unchanged. We may ask you questions about how the app behaved on your device.

Anything you send us is voluntary and specific. Our beta templates deliberately do not ask where you were. Foragers mark spots they do not want found. They ask about terrain and sky instead. Declining any question costs you nothing.

The beta signup list

Separate from the app, and the one place Zynergy Labs holds personal data about you: if you fill in the form at zynergy-labs.com/beta-signup, what you type is stored. This is website data, not app data. The app never sees it, and it is outside the scope of the Play Data safety declaration, which covers the app.

What is stored:

  • Your email address, both as you typed it and lower-cased, the second so that one person signing up twice is recognised as one person.
  • Optionally, a name, a device type, an Android version, and whatever you write in the free-text box. All four can be left empty.
  • A two-letter country code, derived from your connection by Cloudflare rather than asked for, and the date and time you signed up.
  • Whether the notification to us about your signup succeeded, and the error if it did not.

Your IP address is not stored. To stop the form being flooded, a separate table keeps a salted SHA-256 hash of the connecting address with a counter and a timestamp. That is enough to recognise a repeat submission within the hour. Those rows are not deleted automatically.

The list lives in a Cloudflare D1 database we operate, on Cloudflare's infrastructure and under its privacy policy as our processor. When you sign up, a notification carrying what you entered is emailed to Zynergy Labs support through Resend, our mail provider, which processes it in order to deliver it. Nobody else receives it. It is not sold, not shared and not used for any mailing beyond the beta.

How long it is kept: until the beta ends or you ask for it to be removed, whichever comes first. We publish no figure beyond that because there is no automatic expiry that would produce one, and we will not state a retention period we cannot verify.

To have your signup removed, email privacy@zynergy-labs.com. Removing it deletes the record; it does not delete anything on your phone, which was never ours to begin with.

Children

Forager is not directed at children and collects nothing about anyone, including children, beyond what is described above.

Your control over your data

Your journal is stored locally, so you control it directly:

  • Delete items one by one in the app: journal entries, finds, photos, recorded tracks, waypoints, offline regions and planned trips. Deleting a find does not delete its photos; delete those from the app's photo gallery. Crash traces, your last five searches and the cached copy of an exported track cannot be deleted one by one yet; clearing data or uninstalling removes them.
  • Clear all data through Android Settings → Apps → Forager → Storage → Clear data.
  • Uninstall to remove everything. Because Android backup is switched off for this app, there is no copy in your Google account.

We hold no copy of your journal, and no logs of your map tile requests, so for the app's own data there is nothing for us to produce or delete on your behalf.

The exception, and the only one, is the beta signup list. If you joined it, we hold the record described in The beta signup list and can delete it on request: privacy@zynergy-labs.com. Before the signup form existed this paragraph said we held nothing at all about you; that stopped being true the day the form went up, and this is the correction.

Step-by-step detail on deleting everything is at zynergy-labs.com/delete-data, the page named in the Play Data safety declaration.
